# Metric self-attention: design details

This is the second of two documents. It should be read after `project_outline.md`, which gives the question, the background and the shape of the experiment. This document gives the settled decisions and the reasoning behind them. Section 1 is the checklist of what is settled and what is open. Sections 2 to 6 explain the main decisions in turn, and section 7 records the history of the design.

## 1. Design status

This section is the checklist for the design phase. The sections below give the reasoning for some of the settled points.

**Settled** (the user's decisions; dates are 2026-10-05 unless given).
- The question: how head size D and the choice of p interact, studied on the language model alone.
- The arms: the tied dot product as the control, the seven L^p arms (p = 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 8, infinity), and the untied dot product as a reference. This nine-arm grid is confirmed. The uniform control is added to it as a full arm.
- The head-size grid: D = 2, 4, 8, 12 and 16. Only the query-key dimension changes, and the value width stays at 16 per head. D = 1 is skipped.
- Queries and keys are tied in every arm except the untied reference. The diagonal is masked for all arms, and position 0 may attend to itself.
- The score is minus the distance, scaled by a learned factor per head (initial value 1) and a frozen calibrated constant. The constant is chosen so that the standard deviation of the scores equals 1 at initialisation, and it is recomputed for every head size, arm and layer. Matching at initialisation is the whole scheme, and the scale is not tuned per arm.
- The epsilon inside the root is relative to the typical size of the sum inside the root. The ratio is r = 1e-12 (decided 2026-10-05). The p = infinity distance is the largest coordinate difference, with no smoothing.
- No normalisation of queries and keys. Learned absolute positions. The dependence of non-L2 metrics on the coordinate axes is accepted and flagged.
- The model: two layers, width 64, four heads, MLP width four times the width, context 64 characters for every arm, tied input and output embeddings. The corpus is Tiny Shakespeare split 90/10, and the outcome is validation bpc.
- One extra reference arm, the untied dot product without the diagonal mask, is run at D = 16 with the same five seeds (decided 2026-10-05).
- The unmasked untied dot arm is calibrated over all of its allowed keys, which include the position itself (decided 2026-10-05). Its constant is measured by the same procedure as for the other arms.
- The p = 2 distance is computed from direct coordinate differences, as for every other p, and not from the matrix-multiply expansion (decided 2026-10-05).
- Each run takes 4,000 steps (decided 2026-10-05).
- The learning-rate protocol: a one-seed scan at the final length, then each arm at each D is run at its own best rate from the scan. The details are in section 5 (decided 2026-10-05).
- The final weights of every run are saved and committed to git (decided 2026-10-05).
- Five seeds per cell. The uniform control is a full arm with the same five seeds. The runs are made on the laptop (decided 2026-10-05).
- The recall task (MQAR) is shelved, and the language model is run by itself first. The project is restarted from scratch.
- The statistical plan (decided 2026-10-05). Seeds are blocks, and the outcome is validation bpc. There are two primary analyses: the interaction of D and p in a mixed model with the seed as a random effect, and the trend across D of the paired gap between each L^p arm and the tied dot product. The secondary analyses are the paired gap in each cell, contrasts across p and their change with D, and a bootstrap over seeds for the best p. An equivalence test is run only if a claim of equivalence is made, and its margin is deferred. The uniform control is used only as a descriptive scale and is not part of any test. No permutation tests are added, to keep the write-up easy to follow (both decided 2026-10-05). The details are in section 6.

**Open.** Each point will be settled with the user, in an order the user chooses.
- None of the original open points remain. One detail is deferred within the statistical plan: the equivalence margin, which is needed only if test 6 is run.

## 2. Why the diagonal is masked (decided; confirmed 2026-10-05)

With tied projections, a position's query and its own key are the same vector. The distance between them is exactly zero, which is the best score a distance can give, so an unmasked position would favour itself in every layer. For the tied dot product the self-score is the squared vector length, and the Reformer paper (Kitaev, Kaiser and Levskaya, ICLR 2020, section 2) notes that this "will almost always" exceed the score with any other position. That paper is the source of the masking rule and of its exception: a token may not attend to itself unless it has no other valid target, such as the first token. We follow it, so position 0 may attend to itself and every other position may not. The Reformer argument covers only the dot product, and the argument for the distance is the project's own geometric reasoning. A second reason is calibration: the self-key would inflate the measured spread of scores and distort the starting scale. The cost of masking is small, because the residual connection already carries a position's own content to the next layer. The rule applies to all arms in the language model, including the untied dot product, so that the comparison isolates the score function. It does not apply to the recall task, where each query attends only to the context pairs. The write-up must state that this masking departs from a standard language model, so the dot-product baseline is slightly non-standard. An extra reference arm, the untied dot product without the mask, is run at D = 16 to measure the size of this departure.

## 3. Calibration of the score scale (decided; closed 2026-10-05)

Different score functions give different spreads of scores across the keys. The softmax is sensitive to that spread, so an uncorrected comparison would partly measure how sharp each arm's attention happens to be at the start. Each arm therefore multiplies its score by a fixed constant, chosen so that the standard deviation of the scores across a query's allowed keys equals a common target of 1 at initialisation. The constant is measured once for each combination of head size, arm and layer, so it is recomputed for every head size in the experiment. The measurement uses a model built with a fixed calibration seed on fixed batches of text, and all seeds share the result. It excludes the masked diagonal and the early positions with fewer than about 8 allowed keys, and it proceeds layer by layer. A learned scale per head, starting at 1 for every arm, sits on top of the constant, so the model can sharpen or flatten its attention during training. Matching at initialisation is the whole scheme. We do not match the arms later in training, and we do not tune the scale per arm. The target of 1 is a convention that gives soft but not flat attention at the start, and no other target has been tested. A single standard deviation is a crude summary of the score distribution, because distance scores are more skewed than dot-product scores, and this is accepted. The unmasked untied dot arm is the exception to the exclusion of the diagonal. Its allowed keys include the position itself, so it is calibrated over all of them, and the rule on positions with fewer than about 8 allowed keys still applies. The calibrated constant for the tied dot product should land near one over the square root of the head dimension, which serves as a sanity check. The write-up must state that the scale was not tuned per arm and that the target was chosen by convention.

## 4. Epsilon and the p = 2 kernel (decided 2026-10-05)

A small number eps is added to the sum inside the root, so that the gradient stays finite when two vectors coincide. Without it, the gradient is not finite for every p above 1 at an exact match. The number is relative: eps is r times the typical size of the sum for that arm, layer and head size, measured at initialisation and then frozen. The ratio is r = 1e-12. The earlier work found that r = 0 fails, that r = 1e-12 is safe in float32, and that larger values distort the metric for large p. The p = 2 distance uses direct coordinate differences, like every other p. The matrix-multiply expansion is faster, but it lost accuracy when distances are tiny, and the project does not use it. This makes the L2 arm about three times more expensive than it would be with the expansion. The cost estimates already include this. The value r = 1e-12 distorts the metric slightly for large p, because it puts a floor under the distance. By the formula, the floor is the fraction r^(1/p) of the typical distance scale. That is about 3 percent at p = 8, 0.1 percent at p = 4 and 0.0001 percent at p = 2. It is smaller for the other finite p. The p = infinity arm has no epsilon and no floor. The write-up must state the floor for p = 8.

## 5. Learning-rate protocol and training length (decided 2026-10-05)

The earlier work found that the best learning rate can depend on the head size and on the arm, and that a shared rate can manufacture a difference between arms. The protocol therefore tunes the rate separately for every arm and head size, in two stages. The first stage is a scan. Every arm is run at every head size, once each, at the rates 3e-3, 1e-2 and 3e-2, for the full 4,000 steps. The scan seed is not one of the five final seeds. The two extra arms, the uniform control and the unmasked untied dot product, are scanned in the same way. Each arm and head size then takes the rate with the lowest validation bpc. If the best rate is at either end of the grid, that arm and head size is run at one more rate, a factor of 3 beyond the grid. The second stage is the main experiment. Each arm and head size is run for the five final seeds at its chosen rate. The scan is part of the experiment, its results are reported, and the protocol is fixed in advance. The chosen rates and the full scan table go in the write-up. A run that diverges at a rate is reported as a finding, and that rate is not chosen. One seed has noise of about 0.01 bpc. Where two neighbouring rates differ by less than that, the choice is arbitrary but harmless, because the loss is flat there. The write-up must say two things. The first is that each arm's rate was tuned, so the arms differ in more than the score function. The second is that the rate was chosen on the validation set that the results are reported on. The cost is about 8 hours for the scan and about 14 hours for the main runs at 4,000 steps on the laptop, which is about 23 to 24 hours in total with the extra arms. These are estimates with an uncertainty of 30 to 40 percent.

## 6. Statistical plan (decided 2026-10-05; details deferred as listed in section 1)

The design has two varying factors, the head size D and the arm. Each seed is a block: within a seed, all cells share the data stream and the initialisation of the common parameters, so comparisons are paired. The outcome is validation bpc at the final step. The L^p arms are placed on the axis 1/p, with values 1, 0.67, 0.5, 0.33, 0.25, 0.125 and 0 for p = infinity. The two dot-product arms are references and lie off this axis. There are six analyses. Test 1 is a two-way mixed model, bpc ~ D * p, with the seed as a random effect, and the F-test of the D x p interaction. Test 2 is the paired gap between each L^p arm and the tied dot product in each cell, with confidence intervals, and a Holm correction within each D over the family of seven arms. Test 3 is the trend of that gap across D: for each seed and arm, the slope of the gap on log D, then a one-sample t-test on the slopes, and the same test on the gap averaged over arms. Test 4 is a set of contrasts across p, linear and quadratic in 1/p at each D, and then a regression of those contrasts on log D. Test 5 is a bootstrap over seeds, resampling whole seeds, which gives the probability that each p is best at each D and the stability of the ranking across D. Test 6 is an equivalence test (TOST), which needs a margin. The primary analyses are tests 1 and 3. Tests 2, 4 and 5 are secondary and are reported with intervals. Test 6 is run only if the write-up claims that two arms are equivalent, and the margin is chosen then, after the noise has been re-measured. Gaps outside the Holm family are given as uncorrected intervals and are labelled as such. The caveats are that there are only five seeds, that the mixed model assumes approximately normal residuals, and that the spread of bpc may differ across D. The uniform control and the untied dot arms are not entered into any test. The write-up reports the uniform control's bpc beside the other arms as a qualitative scale for how much attention can move the result. The write-up reports each seed's paired difference next to every summary.

## 7. History of the design

These three paragraphs record earlier decisions that shaped the present design.

**Head-size grid (decided 2026-10-05).** The grid of head sizes is D = 2, 4, 8, 12 and 16. The user added D = 8 and D = 12 to the earlier first round of D = 2 and 4 beside the default D = 16.

**Shelving of the recall task (decided 2026-10-05).** The user shelved the recall task (MQAR) because the distance arms sit at its accuracy ceiling and the dot-product result depends on the learning rate, and because further pilots on it would not end. The code, the design decisions and the pilot findings were moved into `shelved/pilot_v1/mqar/`, with its own entry point and tests, and the main engine was made task-agnostic so that the task plugs in. The language model is run by itself first. The full results then decide whether the recall task is needed.

**Restart from scratch (decided 2026-10-05).** The user shelved all of the version 1 code, including the language-model code, the pilot report and the pilot results, in `shelved/pilot_v1/`, and the project is being rebuilt from scratch against the outline and these details. The version 1 design record is `shelved/pilot_v1/DESIGN.md`. It marks which decisions still hold and which were unapproved implementation choices (r = 1e-12 and the direct p = 2 kernel). The grid of nine arms was confirmed on the same day.
